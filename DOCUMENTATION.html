Part I · Product

Turn information overload into focused action

Knowledge workers — founders, operators, senior executives — manage hundreds of emails a week, attend back-to-back meetings, and are expected to track every commitment made across both. The cognitive cost of staying on top of this is enormous.

Chief of Staff connects to your Gmail and Google Calendar and does the work for you. It triages every email with AI, turns the ones that need you into tasks, extracts and de-duplicates the transactions buried in bank alerts and receipts, and answers questions about all of it through a conversational Ask AI interface grounded in your own records. A daily brief lands on the dashboard every morning. Zoom transcripts and commitment tracking are built but not yet switched on — see the status page.

Emails that need the user become tasks, with a due date taken from the email and a priority from its importance. Marketing, alerts, event reminders and repeat nudges are filtered out; one task per thread and per sender's ask. A task from an automated sender closes itself three days after its date.

Part I · Product · Defensibility

Why this is not just an AI wrapper

The most common criticism of AI productivity tools is that they're thin wrappers — paste an API key, prompt an LLM, ship a product. Chief of Staff is architected differently at every layer. The intelligence is not in the prompt; it's in the data infrastructure built around it.

🏗️

A purpose-built data pipeline, not a chat window over your inbox

Every email is parsed, normalised, importance-scored, and routed through a 5-stage worker pipeline long before Ask AI is asked anything about it. Contacts are upserted, financial transactions are extracted and deduplicated, and actionable mail becomes tasks linked to the sender — all asynchronously in a background worker, before you ask a single question. A wrapper sends your raw inbox to GPT. Chief of Staff sends structured, pre-resolved facts.

🧠

The retrieval layer replaces hallucination guards with precision

AI wrappers ask GPT "what did I spend last month?" and hope the answer isn't invented. Chief of Staff classifies the intent, resolves the merchant and date range to DB identifiers, queries transactions_normalized directly, and only then passes verified rows to the LLM as grounded evidence. The model is instructed to answer only from that evidence, but the larger safeguard is upstream: the totals it reports were computed in SQL, not by the model.

🔀

Cross-source reasoning that no single-integration tool can replicate

An email thread with a person, the calendar events they attend and the transactions with a merchant are retrieved by separate, purpose-built queries and ranked against each other in a single answer. People resolve to contacts rows and merchants to canonical names before any query runs. No competitor can reproduce this without rebuilding the same multi-source ingestion, entity resolution, and unified schema — months of infrastructure work, not a prompt tweak.

📈

Precomputed operational state, not real-time inference on every load

Spending spikes, duplicate charges, bills due, overdue tasks, meeting overload and subscription renewals are computed by eight deterministic processors and a synthesis pass that combines their signals — no model call, and not re-inferred from raw text each time you open the dashboard. The operational_insights table is a priority-scored feed that makes the dashboard instant and the Ask AI responses richer. A wrapper has no equivalent; it re-runs inference on every page load.

🔒

Encrypted credentials and database-enforced isolation

OAuth tokens are AES-256-GCM encrypted at rest. Every table carries a Postgres Row-Level Security policy, so a signed-in session can only ever read its own rows. Background work runs with a service-role key that bypasses RLS, so ownership checks in that code carry the isolation instead. Email text is sent to OpenAI for triage and extraction, with quoted replies stripped and the body capped at 3,000 characters; OAuth credentials never leave the server.

The test: If you removed the LLM and replaced it with a different model tomorrow, the product would still work — because the value is in the pipeline, the schema, and the accumulated structured data. The LLM is the final rendering step, not the product.

Part II · Architecture · Ingestion

Ingestion: how data gets in

Two paths write email to communications, and they are not equivalent. Understanding which one is running explains most operational surprises.

A

Live path — Gmail push near real-time

A registered Gmail watch publishes mailbox changes to a Pub/Sub topic; a push subscription forwards them to /api/webhooks/gmail. The route resolves the account, walks Gmail history since the stored cursor, filters to messages with INBOX and without SENT, writes raw_events, and enqueues one job per message.

Five independent things must all hold: the watch must be current (it expires after 7 days and is renewed 4×/day); the refresh token behind that renewal must be alive; the topic must live in the same Google Cloud project as the OAuth client that minted the token; that topic must grant publish rights to gmail-api-push@system.gserviceaccount.com; and a push subscription must point at the live webhook URL. Any one missing produces total silence with no error anywhere — three of these were wrong simultaneously in production.

The refresh token is the one that failed most recently, twice. While the OAuth consent screen sits in Google's Testing status, refresh tokens expire 7 days after issue. Renewal then fails with invalid_grant, the watch lapses, and reconcile correctly reports "no accounts with a live Gmail watch" — every job completes, nothing alerts, and email simply stops. The first occurrence went unnoticed for 18 days. The app is now published to production; reconnecting alone does not fix it, because a token minted under Testing keeps its 7-day life. Since 18 October an invalid_grant marks the integration disconnected and records why, and the dashboard shows a banner when Google is disconnected or no email has been stored for 24 hours.

Because self-sent mail is excluded by design, a self-addressed test email will never produce a raw_event. Testing this path requires genuinely external mail.

B

Bulk path — backfill script historical

npm run backfill:gmail -- --days=N lists messages over a day window (after:N -in:spam -in:trash, so it also picks up sent mail, all stored as inbound), skips anything already stored by external_id, writes communications directly, and enqueues a summarize job. It bypasses raw_events entirely — so the two paths have different failure signatures, and reconciliation has to cover both.

Fetches are serial with a 200 ms pause between messages. In practice a 20-day window of about 1 300 messages took most of a day end to end, because each message is a separate Gmail round-trip plus several database writes. An admin route, POST /api/admin/backfill-gmail, does the same for 90 days across all users, but runs fire-and-forget inside a serverless request, which the platform does not guarantee to finish.

C

Calendar — push channel live

On connect and every 6 hours, events.watch registers a channel on the primary calendar pointing at POST /api/webhooks/calendar, carrying a per-user HMAC token, and stops the channel it replaces. Each notification with a valid token triggers events.list with the stored sync token (first sync: 30 days back to 90 ahead), paging until Google returns the next sync token, and upserts meetings directly in the request — no queue. An expired sync token (HTTP 410) falls back to a full sync. Reconnecting merges into the stored metadata rather than replacing it, so the sync token survives.

D

Zoom and Slack not live

Zoom: POST /api/webhooks/zoom verifies the signature, downloads the VTT transcript on recording.completed and stores it on a meeting row with source = 'zoom' — but nothing creates such rows (Calendar writes google_calendar), and the summary job is enqueued on summarize, whose email handler ignores it. Slack: the webhook verifies requests and writes raw_events to the slack-raw queue, which has no worker.

Trade-off: write-then-enqueue. Both paths commit the database row before enqueueing its job, which means a Redis failure leaves a committed row with no pending work and nothing recording that fact — communications has no status column. The alternative, enqueue-then-write, trades silent data loss for jobs referencing rows that do not exist yet. We kept write-first and added the reconcile pass, because a delayed row is recoverable and a lost one is not.

Part II · Architecture · Processing

Processing: how email becomes structured data

Email is processed by a background worker, never in the request that delivered it: webhooks enqueue BullMQ jobs, and workers run in a separate Railway process with a service-role Supabase client (bypassing RLS). Two jobs carry each email — process-email (stage 1, queue email-raw) and summarize-communication (stages 2–5, queue summarize). Calendar events are the exception: they are written inside the webhook request.

Email processing — 5-stage worker pipeline

1

Fetch & normalise process-email.ts

Gmail API fetches the full message. Headers extracted (from, to, subject, date, threadId, list-unsubscribe). Sender email and name parsed and upserted to the contacts table.

Importance heuristic seeds an importance_score starting at 0.5: +0.2 for Gmail's IMPORTANT label; +0.1 if the To line has a single recipient; +0.15 for urgency keywords (urgent, asap, deadline, invoice, contract, term sheet); and a flat 0.05 if the body mentions "unsubscribe". Clamped to 1. Triage replaces this score in stage 2. The body is stored capped at 10 000 characters, with HTML stripped of <style> and <script> blocks; rendered HTML is never stored.

2

AI triage gpt-4o-mini · max_tokens 700

Calls triageEmail() at temperature 0 with business context, sender, recipients, the new message (quoted reply chain stripped), and a bulk-mail signal — whether the email carries a List-Unsubscribe header — and the date the email was sent, with its weekday, so relative deadlines ("this week", "tomorrow", a date with no year) resolve against it rather than a year the model remembers. The body is declared data: text in it addressed to an AI is ignored. Returns a schema-constrained JSON object with: summary, sentiment (positive / neutral / negative / urgent), refined importance_score, requires_action, action_description, follow_up_deadline, email_category (one of 22), and fallback_category.

Categories are prioritised in a fixed order: Travel > Account Security > Promotions > Finance Bills > Transactions > Subscriptions > Receipts > Legal > Calendar > Shipping > Productivity Tools > Career > Social > … > Other. Promotions is judged by the sender's intent, not the topic: a points-transfer bonus from a hotel chain, a "complete your booking" nudge, or a push to adopt a bank's new login feature is a promotion even though it reads as finance, travel, or security. A renewal or payment notice for something the user already holds — their own insurance policy, subscription, or card — stays in its own category.

3

Financial extraction conditional · gpt-4o-mini

Gate: runs only if the email category is a finance type. Extracts amount, currency, merchant, transaction_type, and confidence. Sets a needs_review flag when the triage category disagrees with the extraction result or confidence is below 0.5.

If review is not needed, calls runDedup(), which groups raw rows describing the same real-world event: amounts within 2%, timestamps within 15 minutes, then matching on transaction ID, reference ID, masked account, or merchant similarity. An order's later emails — shipped, delivered, feedback — arrive days apart, so rows that share a transaction or reference ID (six characters or more) with the same merchant also merge, up to 45 days apart and with amounts within 10%: wide enough for an order whose total shifted between placed and delivered, narrow enough to keep Amazon's per-shipment charges under one order ID separate. The worker's scan fetches those rows by ID, outside its ±24-hour window. A merged group keeps the latest email's amount (the final one) and the earliest email's date (when the order was placed). Refunds never merge with non-refunds, and recurring charges only merge inside the 15-minute window so billing cycles stay distinct. Each group elects a primary by sender priority (BANK > CREDIT_CARD > UPI > WALLET > MERCHANT). The merchant name and category are normalised via the same 100+ alias dictionary used in entity resolution; a known alias always wins over the model's spelling. Aliases shorter than four characters must end at a word boundary, so "VI" no longer claims "Vijay Das". Everywhere merchants are grouped or compared — dedup, spend analysis, Ask AI lookups — they are matched on a key that ignores case and separators, so "washington_post" and "Washington Post" are one merchant.

Extracted dates are clamped on both sides. A date after today falls back to the email's arrival time — extraction sometimes picks up a delivery or due date. So does a date more than 90 days before arrival: Indian bank alerts write DD-MM-YY, which extraction can read as YY-MM-DD, landing a transaction years in the past.

4

Commitment & task extraction gpt-4o-mini · gated

Commitments. extractCommitments() runs only on mail the user sent, never on low-signal categories, and only when requires_action is set; extracted commitments below 0.6 confidence are dropped. A commitment is a promise the user made, so running it on inbound mail extracted other people's words — 317 eligible emails produced zero real commitments. Since sent mail is not yet ingested, email commitment extraction is currently switched off behind a feature flag.

Tasks. Whether an email is a task is a semantic decision, not a score threshold. A task is created when triage sets requires_action and the category allows it: low-signal categories (promotions, newsletters, news, entertainment, social, system notifications, other) never create tasks; transactions and account-security alerts create one only from a human sender, since an automated alert's only "action" is an "if this wasn't you" disclaimer; meetings and learning do the same, since from an automated sender they are event reminders — e-voting windows, AGMs, webinars — that belong on the calendar; and the user's own mail never does. A sender counts as automated if its address matches a no-reply/alerts/updates pattern (one leading segment allowed, as in googleplay-noreply@), the mail carries List-Unsubscribe, or the domain is a known financial sender. The triage prompt separately treats votes, AGMs, webinars and delivery windows as optional rights or events, not obligations, which covers the ones filed under finance. Importance only sets priority ("high" at ≥ 0.85, else "medium"). An expiring gift-card or store-credit balance the user holds is a task, by product decision; a sale or offer that merely mentions gift cards is not. The stored requires_action is this gate's answer rather than triage's raw flag, because the daily brief and the follow-up insights read it: a bank alert blocked as a task must not reach the brief as an email needing action.

A new task is skipped when the same email already has one, when another email in the same thread has an open task, or when the same sender has an open task with a similar title — so nine "renew your car insurance" countdowns from one broker become one task. Skipping rather than replacing preserves anything the user has already acted on.

A pending email task whose due date is more than three days past closes itself (status dismissed) when its source email is automated by the rule above: the vote has closed, the webinar has ended, the gift card has lapsed. Tasks from people, meeting tasks and undated tasks are never closed. The first dry run would close 15 of 17 overdue tasks; the two left are from a person and from expirynotification@qwikcilver.com, which the address pattern does not match.

5

Embed & trigger operational state text-embedding-3-small · 30 s delay

The email summary is chunked (2 048 chars, 200 overlap) and embedded with OpenAI text-embedding-3-small at 1 024 dimensions. The vector is stored on communications.embedding and in memory_chunks. Search runs as exact KNN rather than through an HNSW index: at the current corpus size (~2 000 emails) exact search returns in milliseconds, while an HNSW index over 1 024-dim vectors would consume an estimated 50–100 MB and has to stay memory-resident. The index definitions remain in the schema, commented out, for when the corpus outgrows exact search.

Finally, a compute-operational-state job is enqueued with a 30-second delay and a deduplication key — ensuring batch updates don't trigger redundant recomputes.

Meeting processing pipeline summarize-meeting.ts Not live

Designed to run when a Zoom recording completes; today it never runs, because the job is enqueued on summarize instead of meetings-summarize (see Ingestion). When it runs, the worker parses the stored VTT transcript into speaker turns and calls summarizeMeeting() with the title, attendee list, transcript, and business context. GPT-4o returns a structured JSON containing:

Executive summary

3–5 sentence narrative of what was discussed and decided

Key decisions

Each decision with full context — not just the conclusion

Action items

Owner, due date, and description per item. Items owned by the founder auto-create tasks.

Commitments

Promises extracted with due date and confidence ≥ 0.6 threshold

Follow-up emails

Suggested recipients and topics for post-meeting outreach

Sentiment

productive / tense / unclear / routine — surfaced in the meetings list view

The summary, decisions, and action items are concatenated and embedded (text-embedding-3-small) to memory — making meeting content searchable from Ask AI using semantic queries like "what was decided about pricing in Q1?"

What deliberately does not use a model

Merchant normalisation (a ~100-entry alias dictionary), sender classification, the finance gate, the task gate and its dedup, transaction grouping, retrieval planning, ranking, and all operational-insight generation are pure functions. They are deterministic, free, instant, and unit-testable — and they are where a model would introduce non-determinism for no benefit. When the deterministic normaliser and the model disagree on a merchant, the deterministic answer wins.

Merchant identity. A stored merchant name is a display string, not an identity. Grouping, dedup, spend analysis and Ask AI lookups all compare on merchantKey() — lowercase with separators stripped — so "ZOMATO", "zomato" and "washington_post" / "Washington Post" each resolve to one merchant without rewriting what the model wrote. Alias prefixes under four characters must end at a word boundary; longer ones may still match glued descriptors such as AMAZONIN or paytmqr…@ptys, which is how card and UPI strings usually arrive.

Trade-off: gating over capability. The cheapest way to improve an LLM pipeline's cost profile is usually to stop calling it, not to change models. Skipping commitment extraction for newsletters and promotions cut more cost than any model swap, and cost nothing in quality, because those categories do not contain commitments.

Ask AI: from raw query to resolved entities

When a user types a question, Chief of Staff does not send that text directly to an LLM. POST /api/ask runs a multi-stage pipeline that first understands intent, resolves entities, plans what data to fetch, retrieves it in parallel, then ranks it and asks GPT-4o to answer from the ranked evidence. Every request logs an [ask-trace] line with the intent, plan and sources used. Latency is not currently measured end to end.

1 Intent Classification gpt-4o-mini · temp 0 6 s timeout · blended fallback

The classifier maps every query to one of 12 named intents, extracts structured entities, infers a temporal anchor, and computes a two-dimensional retrieval weight that controls which downstream path activates.

The model names periods reliably but does date arithmetic badly — "this quarter" came back as July–September, "on Friday" as a Wednesday, "the month before" as the same month again — so those phrases are resolved in code (lib/ai/intent/periods.ts) and take precedence: yesterday, tomorrow, next week, this and last quarter, a qualified weekday ("on Friday", "last Monday"; a bare name in passing is ignored), and shifts of the previous turn's period ("the month before", "same period last year"). Rolling windows ("last 30 days") were already computed in code. Months, years and the listed relative tokens stay with the model. "Food" adds groceries to food delivery in code, since the model often returned delivery alone.

Multi-turn context. The answer carries the conversation state — tracked people, merchants, categories and topics with a decaying persistence score, the active date range and intent — as AI SDK message metadata. useChat keeps it on the assistant message and sends it back with the next request, where it is schema-validated (it is client-supplied) and decoded. It is applied only when the new question reads as a follow-up — a pronoun ("did he…"), a "that one" reference, a lead such as "what about" or "and", or a bare time phrase — so "What meetings do I have today?" after a question about Kamal does not inherit Kamal. Follow-ups that swap one slot keep the previous intent, and a named category or merchant replaces its slot: "What about transport?" after a food question asks about transport, not food. The X-Assistant-Context header is still set but nothing reads it.

Design rationale: Pure RAG over embeddings would conflate "how much did I spend on Uber?" with calendar events mentioning Uber. The intent + entity layers route each query to the correct table. The ranking layer then ensures SQL rows, vector matches, and precomputed insights are balanced — not whichever source returned the most rows.

The path forward

The executive assistant software market is large and fragmented. Chief of Staff occupies a defensible position: a tool that combines email, calendar and financial intelligence into one queryable layer. The near-term work is less new capability than making what is built run reliably — the priority-1 and -2 items above — because a confident answer from stale or missing data is worse than no answer. As the platform accumulates longitudinal data per user, the Ask AI feature compounds in value — making it increasingly difficult for single-purpose tools to match the contextual depth of answers Chief of Staff can provide.

The moat is data depth, not features. Every email processed, every task created, every transaction categorised makes future answers more accurate and more personal. The longer a user is on the platform, the better it understands their professional context — relationships, recurring obligations, spending patterns, communication style.
